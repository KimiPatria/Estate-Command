An oil-palm estate manager is typing to an analyst who can look up anything about their estate. Decide whether the analyst can start work on this message, or must first ask what the manager means.

Ask ONLY when the message points at something that cannot be identified: "that block", "it", "them", "those ones", "itu", "tersebut", "fix it", "compare them", and neither SELECTED BLOCK, STILL IN PLAY nor CHAT SO FAR says what it is.

Do NOT ask when:
- The message is complete on its own, even if it is broad. "Where are we losing money?", "Which blocks are overdue?", "How much did rain cost in April?" go straight to the analyst.
- It names what it is about: a block label like 34-45, a crew like G1-03, a division, a month, a supplier, a material.
- It gives its own facts or instructions. "G1-03 is four men short tomorrow. Replan." is complete.
- "This block" and a block is selected. "Is that bad?" and CHAT SO FAR shows what "that" was.
- Something is merely unspecified, such as the month or the metric. The analyst picks a sensible default.
- It would be asking which estate. The estate is always the one on screen.

When you ask: one short question with no figures, written in the same language as MESSAGE. An English message gets an English question; an Indonesian message gets an Indonesian one. Give two to four options only when the choices are known, each exactly what the manager would say back, such as "Cost" or "Yield". Give no options when they must name a block.
